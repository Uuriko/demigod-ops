# Engagement one-pager — draft

**Not published, not sent.** Draft terms for review. Outbound and publishing need
explicit authorisation in the current request.

## Why this exists

Debate outcome (Codex vs Grok, 2026-08-06). Both agents independently reached the
same failure mode from opposite directions:

- **Codex** argued for a 90-day replacement guarantee, then conceded: *"with zero
  placements, Demigod does not yet know whether it can reliably source one good
  hire, much less a replacement under deadline. A guarantee could manufacture
  contingent liability before the delivery engine works."*
- **Grok** argued to run the idle warm-intro path and force one real close, then
  conceded: *"an unsolicited warm intro can be free theater — the founder takes
  the candidate offline and never signs fee or accept."*

Grok wins on sequencing: a guarantee cannot create a meeting; an intro can create
a hire, and a hire creates the first receipt. But Grok's capture problem is real,
and the fix is the *smallest* piece of Codex's proposal — terms agreed **before**
the introduction, not a guarantee product built after it.

This page is that fix. One page, signed before any name moves. It is the thing
that converts Grok's intro from goodwill into a transaction.

## Draft terms

> **Demigod — engagement terms**
>
> **What I do.** I find one person for one role. I read every profile myself. No
> keyword filter, no score, no AI verdict decides who you see.
>
> **Consent.** No candidate's identity reaches you, and your role reaches no
> candidate, until both sides have agreed to the introduction.
>
> **Fee.** [ RATE ]% of first-year base salary, invoiced when the hire starts.
> Nothing upfront. Nothing if you don't hire.
>
> **If it doesn't work out.** If the person leaves or is let go within 90 days of
> their start date, I run one replacement search at no additional fee. Coverage
> starts on the start date and requires the original invoice to be paid. It does
> not cover layoffs, role elimination, or a material change to the role's
> compensation or location.
>
> **Introductions are covered.** If you hire someone I introduced — within 12
> months of the introduction, whether through me or directly — the fee applies.
>
> **What I need from you.** The role, the real constraints, the pay band, and one
> concrete result this person should deliver first.
>
> Signed: ______________________  Date: __________

## Notes on the two blanks

**[ RATE ]** — deliberately blank. The research position: Paraform charges 20–25%
for the same human-recruiter-plus-software model and raised a $40M Series B on it;
Jack & Jill charges 10% but AI agents do the work. Demigod currently charges 10%
for the most manual product in the set. At $180k roles: 10% = $18k (≈11 placements
for $200k); 20% = $36k (≈6). Your call — I'm not putting a number on a live
commercial document.

**90 days** — matches the industry standard band (60/90/120) and Jack & Jill's
3-month full refund. One replacement search, not a cash refund: it is the version
that does not create a cash liability against an unproven delivery engine, which
was Codex's own stated objection to its own proposal.

## The clause that solves Grok's problem

*"Introductions are covered"* — the 12-month capture clause. Without it, the warm
intro is exactly the free theater Grok described: the founder meets the candidate,
hires them directly, and no fee is ever owed. This single clause is what makes the
intro path a business rather than a favour.

It is also the clause a founder is most likely to push back on. Worth deciding in
advance how much you'll narrow it (shorter window, only candidates you sourced,
only for the named role) before you're negotiating live.

## What happens next, per the debate

1. Fill the rate. Sign-ready.
2. Pick one founder with open headcount this week — from the SF map, not the
   sample board.
3. Pick one candidate you can brief in one page.
4. Terms signed → then `demigod-intro-path.mjs` fires. Not before.

stays queued. Grok's point stands: those are product work on a store with zero
customers. They become worth building the moment step 5 happens once.
