# Webflow inspiration deep dive — agencies and portfolios

Research pass: 2026-07-16. Scope: sections 3, 4, and 6 of `docs/WEBFLOW-INSPO-SITES.md`. Notes reflect the live pages where accessible; motion is described at pattern level rather than treated as a feature checklist.

### Refokus — https://www.refokus.com/
- Sector: Digital brand, Web design, Webflow, and creative development agency.
- Hero: Layered, founder-facing tone: “Found. Understood. Chosen.” leads into a long diagnostic H1 about the company outgrowing its brand. Supporting copy is short; contact is the primary CTA rather than a competing pair.
- Nav: Compact top level (Home, Portfolio, About) with Services and Solutions expanded in the menu; one prominent Contact CTA, not dual conversion paths.
- Layout/visual: Dark, cinematic field with huge editorial type, orange eclipse imagery, sound/video, 3D, and scroll-led case studies. Motion is part of the narrative, not decoration around UI cards.
- Proof/trust: Dense testimonials near the top, named case studies, recognizable client work, and a quantified 62 awards plus five agency-of-the-year nominations.
- Steal for Demigod: Use a three-beat micro-line above the dual path—“Matched by tech. Reviewed by humans. Mutual yes.”—then keep the real H1 direct.

### tonik — https://www.tonik.com/
- Sector: Brand, product, and engineering studio for early-stage technology founders.
- Hero: Very short identity H1, “Design studio for visionaries,” followed by one all-caps qualifier for 0→1 founders. No paragraph; trust does the explanatory work immediately below.
- Nav: Roughly four destinations (Work, About, pre-seed sprint, seed fund) plus Contact; one contact action, with audience-specific offers living as links rather than dual CTAs.
- Layout/visual: High-contrast, oversized type and a numbered/showreel rhythm; project imagery and richly art-directed AI/dev-tool cases carry the page. Motion feels portfolio-first and energetic.
- Proof/trust: “Trusted by 200+ Y Combinator & Speedrun founders,” followed immediately by a large logo field including Supabase, LangChain, Clay, Retool, and Twilio.
- Steal for Demigod: Put a single narrow proof strip directly under the hero—one honest number or a few real affiliations—so the two audience CTAs do not need extra sales copy.

### BORING — https://www.weareboring.nl/en
- Sector: Digital experience agency spanning strategy, Webflow/Shopify builds, and ongoing optimization.
- Hero: Punchy lowercase H1, “on-brand web experiences that convert,” with no conventional subhead; small parenthetical labels establish agency type, tenure, and Webflow status.
- Nav: Five content links (Work, Services, About, Insights, Contact) plus a visible “Let’s talk” action; single commercial CTA.
- Layout/visual: Bold editorial typography, playful parenthetical metadata, expressive project showcases, and a confident anti-boring voice. The system is content-heavy but visually grouped into Concept / Build / Optimize.
- Proof/trust: Premium Webflow Partner label, work for named organizations, six years of positioning context, and service specificity rather than generic capability claims.
- Steal for Demigod: Replace a generic eyebrow with three tiny factual tags—“SF,” “10% on hire,” “90-day outcome”—above a minimal dual-path hero.

### Konpo — https://www.konpo.studio/
- Sector: Full-stack design studio covering brand, product, design systems, and websites.
- Hero: Portfolio-led and extremely sparse; the site favors visual greeting, project context, and “Hire us” over a paragraph-length proposition.
- Nav: Four core destinations (Home, About, Work, Contact/Updates) with a separate Hire us action; no dual-audience CTA.
- Layout/visual: Image-dense, experimental studio layout with numeric transitions, playful microcopy, detailed case-study chapters, and a strong sense of scroll choreography.
- Proof/trust: Deep case-study structure (challenge, strategy, branding, product/web design, result) and a qualification form that exposes service, budget, and timeline bands.
- Steal for Demigod: Make the first click itself a lightweight qualifier: the two hero cards should each state who the path is for in one line before “I’m hiring” / “I’m looking.”

### Osmo — https://www.osmo.supply/
- Sector: Webflow and HTML development resources, interaction patterns, courses, and community.
- Hero: Two-line, product-like H1—“Dev Toolkit / Built to Flex”—with a single short sentence listing the resource types. Join is the primary CTA; login is secondary.
- Nav: Product-heavy menu with Login and Join as a clear dual action, though both serve the same audience at different lifecycle stages.
- Layout/visual: Dark utilitarian/editorial shell that demonstrates the product through live-feeling interaction tiles: cursor effects, sliders, transitions, carousels, and easings.
- Proof/trust: The resource previews are the proof; visitors see named, concrete effects before being asked to join. Community and showcase links add social validation.
- Steal for Demigod: Demonstrate “tech + humans” with one restrained matching artifact—a compact candidate signal card beside a visible “human reviewed” stamp—rather than explaining the system in prose.

### Finsweet — https://finsweet.com/
- Sector: Webflow agency plus a broad product and open-source ecosystem.
- Hero: Product/agency positioning is distributed across a dense navigation and modular homepage rather than one poetic claim; offers use short, concrete copy and explicit actions.
- Nav: Large mega-nav organized into Agency, Products, Company, Resources, industries, and capabilities; many links and account/product actions, not a Demigod-scale pattern.
- Layout/visual: Dark, technical, system-led presentation with modular cards and precise product UI. Motion supports an expert/toolmaker identity rather than a cinematic story.
- Proof/trust: Extensive free tools and documentation, named products, precise claims such as sub-100ms search, enterprise/industry capability pages, and visible support surfaces.
- Steal for Demigod: Borrow the specificity, not the mega-nav: under each audience path, name the exact outcome (“review matched candidates” / “join the SF talent pool”) instead of a vague “learn more.”

## Cross-site synthesis for Demigod

- Best-fit pattern: a short mechanism line, direct H1, one-sentence sub, and two equally weighted audience actions.
- Trust belongs immediately after the decision, preferably as factual operating terms or real proof—not a speculative logo wall.
- Agency-grade motion is strongest when attached to meaning. For Demigod, reserve it for the two-path choice and one tech-to-human matching artifact.
- Avoid importing agency mega-navs, sound, showreels, long founder manifestos, or award-wall density. They solve portfolio discovery, not a two-sided talent decision.
- Light or warm-neutral is the safer base for hiring trust; a dark cinematic accent can frame the hero without making the full experience feel like a studio portfolio.
