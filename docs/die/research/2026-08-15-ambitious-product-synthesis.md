# DIE ambitious product synthesis — 2026-08-15

**Status:** non-normative research and product-strategy memo

**Question:** if Demigod borrows the most useful mechanisms from Clay and from adjacent startup
products, what larger product becomes possible, which parts reinforce one another, and how should
the current roadmap test that vision without shrinking it or pretending it is already proven?

**Authority:** this memo proposes a target and experiments. It does not override the
[DIE specification](../../../DEMIGOD-DIE-SPEC.md), [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md), consent rules, source
policies, or current-request action gates. Vendor descriptions below are product evidence, not
independent proof of performance. No live role, candidate, pair, or outcome is inferred here.

**Execution:** the dependency-ordered build plan and current implementation receipt live in
[`IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN-ROLE-MISSION-2026-08-15.md`](../IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN-ROLE-MISSION-2026-08-15.md).

## Executive thesis

DIE should not become “Clay for recruiting.” It should become Demigod's private **company-building
intelligence and relationship system**, beginning with the most demanding complete use case: one
important startup hire.

Its central object is a **Role Mission**: an accepted company need carried from intent through
research, candidate discovery, human review, structured conversations, mutual consent,
introduction, and outcome learning. Every other capability either makes that mission better or does
not belong in the first product.

The useful synthesis is:

```text
Clay's programmable evidence and enrichment
  + Linear's mission and execution hierarchy
  + Hebbia's cited multi-source analysis
  + Glean's permission-aware internal knowledge
  + Affinity's relationship routes
  + Granola's inspectable conversation memory
  + Figma's branching collaboration
  + Vanta's continuous evidence assurance
  + Persona and Ramp's review and exception controls
  + Cal.com's coordination
  + Pave's compensation context
  + Paraform's expert network
  + Braintrust and PostHog's evaluation loop
  + Temporal's durable execution
  = one human-governed system for turning a startup need into a trusted relationship
```

This is one product, not a suite of disconnected clones. The durable advantage is the joined
history of what the company needed, what evidence was available, what people corrected, which
relationship route was permitted, what both sides consented to, and what happened afterward.

The current roadmap already contains most required primitives through R10. Its gap is not feature
count. Its gap is a unifying product model, an explicit authority plane, and a research portfolio
that can distinguish a reinforcing system from a bag of integrations.

## 1. What changes—and what does not

### 1.1 The ambition expands

The narrow initial evidence sidecar remains a valid kernel, but it is not the whole destination.
DIE can eventually help Demigod:

- define and maintain a startup's actual hiring mission;
- join company truth, internal context, market evidence, and consented talent context;
- coordinate operators, founders, candidates, recruiters, and bounded agents;
- discover warm, trusted routes instead of treating people as rows in a contact database;
- preserve conversation evidence and disagreement without turning notes into a verdict;
- prepare every approved handoff from research through scheduling and offer context;
- evaluate which evidence, questions, channels, and interventions were actually useful;
- generalize the same primitives to advisors, customers, investors, and other company-building
  relationships only after the hiring mission proves them.

### 1.2 The invariants remain

- Evidence informs; it does not make an employment decision.
- People are participants with rights, preferences, corrections, and consent—not enrichment targets.
- Public research cannot see private context or acquire write, send, publish, spend, consent, or
  introduction authority.
- A warm path is derived only from permitted relationship data and human-entered knowledge.
- Unknown, conflict, stale, quarantined, suppressed, and error remain distinct.
- Prepared work is visibly different from executed work.
- A simulation may prove mechanics; only a real accepted role and real observed loop can prove
  product value.

### 1.3 Reconcile the dated research briefs

The July shared brief describes the smallest company-evidence wedge and still protects that shipped
boundary. The August v2 specification and roadmap intentionally expand toward role, candidate,
relationship, workflow, and outcome capabilities with stricter trust zones. Read them as stages,
not as competing product definitions:

```text
company-evidence kernel
  → role-centered operating surface
  → permissioned talent and relationship intelligence
  → controlled coordination
  → outcome-linked learning
  → reusable company-building intelligence primitives
```

## 2. The product: Role Mission OS

### 2.1 The unit of value

A Role Mission begins when an accepted role has an accountable owner, desired 90-day outcome,
must-haves, constraints, and interview plan. It ends only when it is closed with an observed state:
filled, paused, changed, abandoned, or unresolved. It preserves the history between those points.

The mission is useful because it gives every participant and function the same scoped context while
preserving different permissions. Company research, candidate evidence, interview notes,
relationship paths, and action previews can meet in one mission without collapsing into one trust
zone or one score.

### 2.2 The eight product surfaces

#### A. Role Mission Control

The shared operational home for the role:

- outcome, must-haves, constraints, owner, status, and version history;
- readiness checkpoints and unresolved questions;
- linked projects, research runs, interviews, approvals, and next safe actions;
- a health narrative derived from explicit state, not a decorative traffic light;
- timeline of scope changes and their downstream impact.

Borrowed mechanism: Linear's initiatives/projects/issues hierarchy, adapted so mission intent and
evidence are first-class rather than hidden in tasks.

#### B. Evidence Matrix

Each role question is crossed with relevant company, market, and candidate evidence:

- supported, conflict, unknown, stale, suppressed, or not-applicable state;
- exact source span, source identity, time context, use basis, and reviewer correction;
- research cells that can run bounded functions or waterfalls;
- comparison of claims without a global fit score;
- visible dependencies showing which mission conclusions would change if a source changes.

Borrowed mechanism: Clay's tables/functions/waterfalls plus Hebbia's cited multi-document analysis.

#### C. Company Time Machine

A bitemporal view of the company and role context:

- what the source said, when it was observed, and when the fact was believed valid;
- funding, product, team, hiring, role, and market changes;
- exact historical context used during a review;
- material-change alerts linked to affected claims and mission questions.

Borrowed mechanism: Clay account agents and Vanta continuous monitoring, with evidence drift and
human review instead of silent field replacement.

#### D. Relationship Router

A permissioned map of credible paths between company, candidate, operator, recruiter, and trusted
participants:

- direct, referred, prior-conversation, prior-pair, and expert-curated routes;
- source, recency, strength evidence, owner, permitted use, and do-not-contact state;
- path comparison by trust and appropriateness, not merely shortest graph distance;
- a proposed introduction packet whose two endpoints remain independently consent-gated.

Borrowed mechanism: Affinity relationship intelligence, narrowed to owned, consented, or explicitly
entered relationship context.

#### E. Opportunity Room

A private, role-scoped collaboration space:

- founder calibration and operator interpretation kept as distinct contributions;
- candidate questions and corrections represented in their own voice;
- comments anchored to claims or requirements;
- alternative research or review branches that can be compared and merged;
- decisions with rationale, dissent, evidence consulted, and supersession history.

Borrowed mechanism: Figma branches/review/merge plus Granola's distinction between source moment,
AI-enhanced notes, and user-authored notes.

#### F. Mutual-Consent Exchange

The controlled transition from internal work to a relationship:

- role, company, compensation, process, privacy, and context visible before consent;
- founder and candidate consent tracked independently;
- exact introduction preview, purpose, recipients, expiry, and withdrawal state;
- scheduling, reminders, preparation, decline, and close-loop state;
- stop conditions that suppress pending work immediately.

Borrowed mechanism: Persona's progressive verification and cases, Ramp's recommendation/review
boundary, and Cal.com's routing and scheduling workflows.

#### G. Talent Research Network

Human experts and bounded agents operate through the same mission contract:

- scoped research bounties or assignments;
- visible questions, permitted sources, delivery format, deadline, and budget;
- sample-first work and evidence quality review;
- contributor strengths learned from accepted evidence and corrections;
- no participant gains broader person-data or outbound authority from an assignment.

Borrowed mechanism: Paraform's expert marketplace, Retool's data-connected internal tools, and
Clay's reusable research functions.

#### H. Learning and Trust Center

The product's measurement and governance surface:

- which evidence was shown, consulted, corrected, or ignored;
- source/function quality by field and mission cohort;
- review burden, clarification loops, stage duration, candidate experience, and outcomes;
- policy exceptions, approvals, incidents, retention, and deletion state;
- replayable eval cases and product experiments that preserve original human judgment.

Borrowed mechanism: Braintrust's observe/annotate/evaluate loop, PostHog's product experimentation,
and Vanta's continuous control evidence.

## 4. One coherent end-to-end journey

### Stage 1 — Accept and calibrate the mission

1. An accepted role anchors the mission to a canonical company and responsible owner.
2. Founder-authored outcome, must-haves, constraints, compensation context, and process remain
   distinguishable from operator interpretation and public evidence.
3. DIE identifies ambiguity and conflicts; it drafts questions rather than silently resolving them.
4. A mission is not search-ready until the human-owned criteria and source boundaries are visible.

### Stage 2 — Build the evidence matrix

1. Existing accepted evidence is reused before new retrieval.
2. Each requirement becomes explicit evidence questions.
3. Company, market, and role context is retrieved through field-specific source policy.
4. Unknowns and disagreements become work items in Mission Control.
5. Material changes update affected cells, never the historical review snapshot.

### Stage 3 — Form the permitted talent pool

1. Inbound, consented rediscovery, prior pairs, referrals, recruiter-curated, and permitted external
   channels remain visibly separate.
2. Exact identity and suppression run before enrichment or review prioritization.
3. Deterministic hard constraints narrow the set.
4. Evidence questions produce cited comparisons and missing-information prompts.
5. A capped active review queue prevents abundance from becoming unbounded operator work.

### Stage 4 — Collaborate and investigate

1. Reviewers inspect the same evidence matrix but retain distinct notes and judgments.
2. A disagreement can open a branch: alternative requirement interpretation, missing research, or
   proposed interview question.
3. Consented conversations add source-linked notes and corrections.
4. No summary changes the underlying evidence or pair state by itself.

### Stage 5 — Establish mutual intent

1. Each side receives the context appropriate to it before consent.
2. Founder and candidate consent are independent, scoped, time-bounded events.
3. The Relationship Router proposes the best permitted route and explains why.
4. The exact introduction, scheduling, and preparation plan is previewed.
5. Changed role truth, reply, decline, opt-out, or withdrawal halts pending action.

### Stage 6 — Learn from the observed outcome

1. The system records process events without pretending they prove causality.
2. It preserves what evidence and policy existed at decision time.
3. Reviewers can mark evidence shown, consulted, corrected, decision-relevant, or misleading.
4. Source selection, questions, and routing policy can improve prospectively.
5. Historical evidence and human judgments are never rewritten to flatter the outcome.

## 5. Conceptual object model

This is a product vocabulary, not a new database contract. Existing canonical Demigod entities and
stores should satisfy it before any new storage or abstraction is added.

| Object | Purpose | Must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| `RoleMission` | scoped journey for one accepted company need | acceptance, consent, or action authority merely by existing |
| `Requirement` | founder-authored or reviewed criterion, constraint, outcome, or process rule | model-generated truth |
| `Question` | explicit uncertainty whose answer may help a review | compulsory person-data collection |
| `EvidenceClaim` | typed, sourced, time-aware answer or unknown/conflict | verdict or hidden confidence |
| `ResearchRun` | versioned inputs, tools, outputs, cost, failures, and receipt | permission to persist or act on every output |
| `CompanyContext` | company and market evidence relevant to the mission | current facts back-projected into history |
| `ConsentedPersonContext` | submitted or permitted person evidence and preferences | public people database |
| `RelationshipRoute` | permitted path with source, owner, recency, and purpose | permission to contact or introduce |
| `Conversation` | consented source moment, notes, correction, and summary | an employment decision |
| `ReviewBranch` | alternative interpretation or proposed change | duplicate canonical truth |
| `DecisionRecord` | human choice, rationale, dissent, and consulted evidence | causal proof or irreversible model label |
| `Consent` | actor-, purpose-, scope-, and time-specific permission | consent by proxy or for another action |
| `ActionPreview` | exact proposed external mutation or communication | execution |
| `Introduction` | authorized handoff between two mutually consenting endpoints | ongoing outreach permission |
| `OutcomeEvent` | observed process or relationship result | a ground-truth label for future candidates |
| `Experiment` | predeclared change, cohort, metric, guardrail, and result | unrestricted optimization |
| `PolicyException` | uncertain or out-of-policy case routed to a named reviewer | silent policy bypass |

## 6. Authority and permission planes

Ambition fails if one giant agent gains every tool. DIE instead needs capabilities that meet at the
mission through explicit contracts.

| Plane | Can read | Can propose | Cannot do without separate authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public research | allowlisted public sources and public-safe company/role context | claims, conflicts, questions | see private candidate data, write, message, publish, spend |
| Private company operations | accepted role and authorized internal company context | calibration, correction, mission state changes | disclose candidate context or act externally |
| Candidate context | submitted/consented data and permitted work evidence | corrections, preferences, review questions | become public output or train an employment verdict |
| Relationship context | owned or explicitly entered touch and route data | warm path and intro preview | infer private relationships, send, or consent for either endpoint |
| Conversation | consented recording/notes within retention scope | summary, evidence spans, follow-ups | change pair state or make employment decision |
| Action | exact approved preview and minimum target context | execution receipt and reconciliation | broaden target, payload, budget, or later sequence steps |
| Evaluation | redacted traces, labels, outcomes, and cohorts | prospective routing or product change | rewrite history or infer protected traits |

## 7. The three strategic bets

### Bet A — Role Mission OS

**Hypothesis:** founders and operators get more value from one complete mission surface than from a
better standalone sourcing, research, ATS, or note-taking feature.

**Why it could win:** the mission retains intent and evidence across tools that normally fragment
the loop. That lowers repeated calibration, lost context, and premature automation.

**What would falsify it:** participants keep working in separate tools because the mission surface
does not reduce a clarification loop, prevent an error, accelerate review, or improve a handoff.

### Bet B — Trust and Relationship Network

**Hypothesis:** for important startup hires, trusted routes and excellent mutual context outperform
anonymous volume as the main path to a successful introduction.

**Why it could win:** Demigod can combine human curation, consent, relationship history, startup
truth, and role-specific evidence without becoming a contact scraper.

**What would falsify it:** permitted relationship paths rarely change response, mutual interest,
speed, or candidate experience relative to ordinary direct outreach.

### Bet C — Compounding Hiring Intelligence

**Hypothesis:** corrections and observed decisions linked to the exact evidence available at the
time can improve future questions, sources, and workflow routing without learning a global fit
score.

**Why it could win:** most systems record stages and outcomes but lose the epistemic chain—what was
known, uncertain, corrected, and actually consulted.

**What would falsify it:** outcomes are too sparse, incomparable, or confounded to improve even
narrow prospective policies, or the annotation burden exceeds the value.

## 8. Research portfolio

The research program should pursue disconnected mechanisms in parallel, then look for joins that
improve a Role Mission. It should not ask only “which recruiting feature is popular?”

### 8.1 Mission definition and organizational intent

1. How do incident command, military mission command, and surgical checklists preserve intent while
   letting execution adapt?
2. Which parts of product requirements, job scorecards, and strategy deployment translate cleanly
   into a role mission?
3. How should requirement changes invalidate prior research, filters, interview plans, and consent?
4. What is the smallest mission health model that predicts real stuckness better than status labels?
5. Which ambiguities should generate a founder question rather than an agent assumption?

### 8.2 Evidence and decision science

6. Which evidence presentation reduces error without increasing automation bias?
7. When do citations improve verification, and when do they merely increase perceived authority?
8. How should evidence value be estimated before paying for another source?
9. Which forms of disagreement are informative versus noise?
10. How can a reviewer preserve an independent initial judgment before seeing an AI synthesis?
11. How should missingness and source conflict affect review order without becoming a fit score?

### 8.3 Temporal and causal reasoning

12. Which company facts need valid-time history rather than only observation time?
13. How should role scope changes propagate without rewriting historical review packets?
14. What natural experiments could estimate whether an evidence intervention helped?
15. Which outcome claims must remain descriptive because selective labels make causal inference
    unsafe?
16. How can the product detect performative effects—people changing behavior because the system
    measures or recommends something?

### 8.4 Relationship and network science

17. What makes an introduction path trusted: direct knowledge, recency, mutual context, domain
    credibility, or explicit willingness?
18. When is a longer human path more appropriate than a shorter graph path?
19. How should relationship strength be represented without private-message surveillance?
20. Which network effects improve matching quality without pressuring people to expose contacts?
21. How should intro debt, reciprocity, and repeated asks be bounded to avoid extractive behavior?

### 8.5 Markets and mechanism design

22. How should two-sided preferences be elicited without revealing sensitive information too early?
23. Which marketplace rules prevent recruiter spam, duplicate submissions, and race-to-contact?
24. Can research bounties reward accepted evidence rather than candidate quantity?
25. Which forms of congestion, unraveling, and strategic signaling appear in startup hiring?
26. How should an option to wait, gather information, or decline be represented as a positive state?

### 8.6 Human expertise and services

27. Which recruiter tasks are judgment, which are coordination, and which are repeatable functions?
28. What context lets an expert contribute narrowly without exposing the whole mission?
29. How should expert quality be learned across different role and company cohorts?
30. Where does a human concierge create disproportionate trust compared with self-serve software?
31. Which candidate-preparation interventions are helpful without coaching people into sameness?

### 8.7 Privacy, fairness, and consent

32. What is the minimum data required at each progressive stage of the relationship?
33. How can candidate correction, deletion, retention, and withdrawal propagate through derived
    claims and queued actions?
34. Which apparently neutral startup or career proxies can recreate protected-trait discrimination?
35. How should the system measure fairness when outcomes are sparse and selection is endogenous?
36. What candidate-visible explanation is useful without exposing another person's private notes?

### 8.8 Collaboration and knowledge systems

37. When do branches improve role calibration, and when are comments enough?
38. How can decisions preserve dissent without creating bureaucratic approval theater?
39. Which knowledge should be mission-local, company-wide, operator-private, or candidate-visible?
40. How should meeting source moments, human notes, AI summaries, and later corrections coexist?
41. What makes a mission genuinely portable between operators without flattening tacit knowledge?

### 8.9 Workflow reliability and operations

42. Which long-running processes actually require durable workflow infrastructure?
43. What stop events must interrupt scheduling, research, sequences, or writeback immediately?
44. How should retries behave when the external world may already have changed?
45. Which policy decisions can be deterministic and which must become exception cases?
46. What evidence demonstrates that an integration reduces work instead of adding reconciliation?

### 8.10 Product economics and growth

47. Is the economic unit a role, active mission, successful introduction, retained relationship, or
    expert hour?
48. Which part of the system creates willingness to pay before a hire occurs?
49. Does a high-touch service produce data that improves software, or does it conceal weak software?
50. Which adjacent use case—advisor, customer, investor, design partner, or executive network—reuses
    the Role Mission primitives with the least distortion?
51. What is the smallest loop that produces a real data network effect rather than a database-size
    marketing claim?

## 9. Roadmap audit

The canonical roadmap is ambitious enough. It should not be replaced. The useful change is to make
each stage serve the integrated product and attach a sharper product question.

| Current stage | Role Mission contribution | Product question it must answer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 kernel | truth, identity, clocks, authority, receipts | can the product refuse unsupported state reliably? | treating safety gates as product proof |
| R1 company intelligence | Company Time Machine seed | does company evidence change or clarify a mission question? | generic company database expansion |
| R2 role workspace | Mission Control shell | can one surface remove a real context or coordination break? | universal table builder before one full role works |
| R3 research runtime | Evidence Matrix engine | can a bounded function produce a useful cited answer at known cost? | provider marketplace or agent spectacle |
| R4 memory/signals | Time Machine and dynamic attention | does a material change identify the exact mission decision at risk? | noisy alerts or person surveillance |
| R5 talent intelligence | permitted pool, pair evidence, interviews | can evidence improve human review while preserving uncertainty and agency? | global score or autonomous rejection |
| R6 integrations | permission and connector plane | can outside systems share state without duplicate truth or privilege escalation? | integration count as strategy |
| R7 controlled action | Mutual-Consent Exchange | can an approved handoff be faster and safer with reliable stop conditions? | autonomous sequences |
| R8 learning | Learning and Trust Center | can corrections improve a narrow prospective policy? | causal or fairness claims from sparse funnel data |
| R9 workflow builder | mission authoring and reuse | can an operator safely reuse a proven workflow without hidden privilege? | natural-language magic before stable primitives |
| R10 scale | durable multi-user substrate | which measured failure requires a database, queue, index, or tenant boundary? | platform rewrite in anticipation of scale |

### 9.1 Sequencing implication

The next product slice should cross R2, R3, and R5 vertically rather than completing every generic R2
or R3 feature horizontally. The roadmap's current “R2 → R5 evidence corpus” pointer is correct. It
should be understood as the first thin Role Mission, not merely candidate enrichment.

### 9.2 Capability clusters, not isolated epics

The roadmap can be mentally grouped into four reinforcing clusters:

```text
Mission and evidence:      R0 + R1 + R2 + R3
People and relationships:  R4 + R5
Consent and coordination:  R6 + R7
Learning and scale:        R8 + R9 + R10
```

Each cluster has a complete user outcome and a permanent authority boundary. Infrastructure enters
only when the next cluster's measured failure requires it.

## 10. First ambition-preserving product proof

Build and evaluate a **Role Mission Decision Trace** using the existing role workspace and frozen or
explicitly synthetic data until a real accepted role is available.

### 10.1 Scope

For one mission, show:

1. accepted/synthetic role intent and versioned requirements;
2. company packet and mission-relevant company claims;
3. one explicit evidence question per must-have;
4. permitted candidate evidence or clearly labeled fixture unknowns;
5. reviewer notes kept separate from retrieved claims;
6. an initial review, consulted-evidence markers, correction, and final human decision trace;
7. readiness and authority state, with consent and external actions unavailable in simulation.

This is a vertical product test, not a new data model or provider integration. Existing commands,
stores, role workspace projections, and fixture isolation should be reused.

### 10.2 What it tests

- Whether one mission makes the fragmented context easier to understand.
- Whether the Evidence Matrix exposes a consequential unknown or conflict.
- Whether the reviewer can distinguish source claim, agent synthesis, human note, and decision.
- Whether a changed requirement identifies which research and candidate comparisons are stale.
- Whether safety and permission boundaries remain visible without dominating the workflow.

### 10.3 Acceptance checks

- Every material displayed claim has source, state, and time context or is explicitly human-authored.
- A requirement change marks dependent answers for review without changing historical snapshots.
- Empty or conflicted evidence remains visible and cannot become a positive claim.
- Candidate fixture data cannot enter public/company output or real queues.
- Research output cannot change pair, consent, intro, or action state.
- A reviewer can reconstruct the decision trace without opening raw JSON.

### 10.4 Product-value gate

The simulation can establish coherence and safety only. Product value requires one real accepted
role where the trace records at least one concrete effect:

- exposed a missing question or source conflict;
- prevented a mistaken assumption;
- changed or clarified a human review;
- reduced a clarification loop or research burden;
- improved the mutual-consent or introduction packet.

If repeated real missions show none of these, more providers, agents, and surfaces do not rescue the
thesis.

## 11. Research and execution cadence

Use a portfolio rather than one giant discovery phase:

1. **Frame:** state the mission problem, weakest current hypothesis, plausible alternatives, and
   disconfirming evidence.
2. **Scan:** search outside recruiting for mechanisms in decision science, workflow reliability,
   collaboration, trust, markets, and professional services.
3. **Translate:** write the Demigod-specific mechanism, trust boundary, and earliest proof; discard
   superficial feature resemblance.
4. **Prototype:** use the existing role workspace and fixtures to test one interaction or state
   transition.
5. **Evaluate:** record task success, error, burden, correction, and qualitative surprise; do not
   substitute engagement metrics.
6. **Promote:** change the canonical roadmap or contracts only after the result survives review.
7. **Learn:** connect later real outcomes prospectively without rewriting the original evidence.

A research note is complete only when it names a decision it could change. A product experiment is
complete only when it names a falsifier and preserves a replayable receipt.

## 12. What else: mechanisms from distant domains

Recruiting software mostly optimizes sourcing, workflow, and funnel reporting. The larger opportunity
appears when DIE borrows from fields that manage incomplete information, high-consequence handoffs,
multiple authorities, long-lived evidence, and two-sided trust.

| Distant domain | Useful mechanism | Role Mission translation | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency and incident command | common operating picture, named command, resource/state visibility, explicit handoffs | one mission picture with owner, role truth, active work, blocked decisions, and next authorized action | hiring is not an emergency; do not create urgency theater or militarize the candidate experience |
| Aviation crew resource management | assertive dissent, conflict resolution, shared mental model, self-critique | structured reviewer dissent and pre-decision challenge before a consequential recommendation | a checklist cannot validate a hiring criterion or erase power differences |
| Clinical shared decision-making | professional evidence combined with participant preferences and values | founder and candidate each see relevant evidence, express preferences, ask questions, and retain decision agency | employment is not healthcare; borrow the participation pattern, not clinical authority |
| Cybersecurity case management | alerts become scoped cases with evidence, actions, comments, ownership, and closure conditions | one hiring mission as an investigation and coordination case rather than a loose project | candidates are not threats or incidents; language and UI must remain respectful |
| Data engineering | assets, jobs, runs, lineage, materialization, impact analysis | claims, packets, functions, and reviews carry an evidence bill of materials and downstream impact graph | do not introduce a data platform merely to draw lineage |
| Scientific research | versioned protocols, reproducible runs, preregistered method, correction history | reusable role-research protocols with exact version, inputs, deviations, and result | human careers are not lab specimens; protocolization must not flatten judgment |
| Digital twins and scenario planning | fork the current state, apply hypothetical actions, compare consequences | test a changed requirement, location policy, compensation band, or source strategy without mutating the live mission | scenarios are conditional, not predictions or authority |
| Financial data linking | the person initiates a connection, authenticates at the source, selects scope, and can reconnect | candidate-controlled import of selected profile, portfolio, calendar, or employment evidence | never request credentials or imply that linked data is mandatory |
| Consent receipts | human-readable, machine-readable record of purpose, data, controller, and time | every person-data grant and introduction permission returns a receipt that can be inspected and withdrawn | a receipt does not repair coercive, bundled, or invalid consent |
| Authorization policy | principal, action, resource, and context evaluated separately with default deny | exact actor × operation × mission object × current context check before every sensitive read or action | policy-as-code is later substrate; current explicit gates remain sufficient until complexity requires more |
| Contract lifecycle management | launch form, redlines, approvals, counterparty turn, signature packet, archive | an offer and introduction room that preserves alternatives, approvals, and which side owes the next step | an offer is not a standard contract workflow; legal and human nuance remain external authority |
| Hospitality CRM | preferences, history, recognition, continuity, and recovery from a poor experience | remember how a candidate wants to interact and avoid making returning people repeat context | preferences must be volunteered, useful, expiring, and never used for covert persuasion |
| Forecasting tournaments | explicit questions, probabilistic predictions, resolution criteria, and track records | calibrate forecasts about process duration, research yield, or role-change risk—not candidate worth | no candidate leaderboard, gamified employment decision, or unvalidated proxy assessment |
| Privacy-enhanced collaboration | joint aggregate analysis with query/output controls and no raw-data exchange | market supply, compensation, or process benchmarks across trusted firms without exposing individuals | a future research direction only; privacy technology does not create a lawful purpose by itself |
| Customer research repositories | source moments → tagged observations → synthesized findings → product decisions | conversations and corrections remain traceable to mission questions and workflow changes | tags and summaries must not become permanent labels on people |
| AI risk management | govern, map, measure, manage; roles and human-AI configurations explicitly documented | risk-tier functions by data, consequence, reversibility, and human oversight | a governance framework must change behavior and tests, not become a compliance document pile |

### 12.1 The common pattern across these domains

The shared mechanism is not “more automation.” It is a **case with a common operating picture**:

```text
purpose and owner
  + scoped participants and permissions
  + evidence and provenance
  + unresolved questions and alternative interpretations
  + explicit actions and stop conditions
  + handoffs and consent
  + closure state and retrospective
```

That is a stronger product definition for a Role Mission than either a recruiting pipeline or a
generic data table. It explains why Clay-like evidence, Linear-like work, Affinity-like relationships,
and Persona-like controls belong together without claiming that every mechanism must ship.

## 13. Novel compound product concepts

These are not roadmap commitments. Each combines mechanisms that are usually sold in separate
categories and names the weakest test that could justify further work.

### 13.1 Mission Twin

**Combination:** Palantir scenarios + Figma branching + Linear initiatives + DIE time-aware evidence.

Create a reversible fork of a live Role Mission. A founder or operator can ask, “What changes if the
role becomes hybrid, the compensation band moves, this must-have becomes learnable, or the 90-day
outcome changes?” The fork recomputes affected questions, pool eligibility, interview plan, and stale
evidence without modifying live state.

**Why it is new:** hiring systems usually edit the requisition in place, losing the counterfactual.
The Mission Twin treats role design as a scenario that can be compared before commitment.

**First test:** on fixture data, change one requirement and show only the genuinely dependent claims,
filters, and questions as affected. A manually written impact list is sufficient; no ontology engine.

### 13.2 Candidate Data Link

**Combination:** Plaid Link + Kantara consent receipts + Persona progressive verification + DIE
candidate protections.

Let a candidate initiate a connection to a specific source, select what to share for a stated mission,
inspect the normalized result, correct it, and receive a revocable receipt. The candidate is the data
controller in the interaction rather than the target of an enrichment query.

Possible sources include a portfolio, submitted documents, selected calendar availability, verified
employment evidence, or a professional profile export. The mechanism matters more than connector
count.

**First test:** a static consent and correction prototype using synthetic data. It must make scope,
purpose, retention, recipients, expiry, and withdrawal consequences understandable.

### 13.3 Evidence Bill of Materials

**Combination:** W3C PROV + OpenLineage + Dagster asset thinking + Clay research runs.

Every review packet carries a compact manifest of the entities, source observations, functions,
models, human corrections, and activities that produced it. If a source is retracted or a function is
found defective, DIE can identify exactly which mission artifacts require review.

**Why it is new:** citations answer “where did this sentence come from?” A bill of materials also
answers “what process transformed it, who corrected it, which decisions consumed it, and what is now
at risk?”

**First test:** derive the manifest from existing packet/run receipts for one fixture. Do not create a
general graph store.

### 13.4 Hiring Case and Control Tower

**Combination:** incident.io + Tines Cases + FEMA common operating picture + Front shared queues.

Represent each active mission as a case with owner, current objective, active uncertainties, pending
handoffs, next safe action, closure conditions, and retrospective. A portfolio view shows attention
needs across missions without flattening them into funnel counts.

**First test:** determine whether the existing readiness projection can distinguish waiting on founder,
waiting on evidence, waiting on candidate, paused, and genuinely actionable work. Do not add alerts
until a missed handoff is observed.

### 13.5 Decision Rehearsal

**Combination:** aviation crew resource management + clinical shared decision-making + structured
analytic techniques + Braintrust evals.

Before a consequential recommendation, preserve the reviewer's independent view, surface the best
counterargument, name missing evidence, and ask what observation would change the decision. Later,
compare the initial view, AI-assisted view, group decision, and outcome without declaring the outcome a
ground-truth label.

**First test:** add a fixture-only review form with four prompts: initial view, contrary evidence,
decision-changing evidence, and final rationale. Measure burden and whether it produces a new question.

### 13.6 Mutual Diligence Room

**Combination:** Ironclad counterparty workflows + Harvey source-backed professional work + Granola
conversation memory + Demigod mutual consent.

Give founder and candidate a shared, purpose-limited room containing only mutually appropriate facts:
role outcome, company context, process, compensation assumptions, open questions, scheduled steps, and
agreed corrections. Private evaluation notes remain outside. Each side can propose clarification without
gaining access to the other's private workspace.

**Why it is new:** candidate portals usually expose status; this exposes the shared substance required
for an informed mutual decision.

**First test:** produce two projections from one fixture mission—operator-private and mutual—and prove
that private notes cannot enter the latter.

### 13.7 Offer Scenario Studio

**Combination:** Carta compensation/equity benchmarks + Pave workflows + Palantir scenarios + Ironclad
version comparison.

Compare salary, equity, vesting, location, start date, scope, and milestone assumptions as explicit
scenarios. Show sources, valuation assumptions, dilution uncertainty, and which party proposed each
change. The studio explains tradeoffs; it does not recommend the lowest acceptable package or infer a
candidate's reservation price.

**First test:** a sourced scenario packet with three intentionally different assumptions and no
optimization. Validate that the reader can identify what drives the difference.

### 13.8 Relationship Hospitality Layer

**Combination:** SevenRooms guest continuity + Affinity relationship history + Front service recovery.

Store volunteered interaction preferences, prior context, commitments, and recovery needs so repeat
participants do not feel like anonymous rows. Examples: preferred channel, scheduling constraints,
accessibility needs, what context they do not want repeated, and whether they welcome future relevant
roles.

**First test:** determine whether consented prior participants value continuity and correction. Retain
only preferences that change service and expire them by policy.

### 13.9 Research Protocol Registry

**Combination:** protocols.io + Clay functions + Braintrust datasets + Vanta control evidence.

Turn the best company, role, and talent research practices into versioned protocols: question, allowed
sources, steps, stopping rule, expected output, review rule, cost class, and known failure modes. Runs
record deviations instead of pretending every researcher followed the method exactly.

**First test:** express one existing company-research function as a protocol and replay it against the
frozen benchmark. The registry is a document/index until repeated execution needs software.

### 13.10 Mission Forecast Ledger

**Combination:** Metaculus resolution discipline + incident retrospectives + DIE outcome events.

Operators can make a few calibrated forecasts about the process: probability the role changes within
30 days, probability a missing question blocks review, expected time to mutual decision, or expected
yield of a research channel. Each forecast has a resolution rule and is scored later to improve
operational calibration.

**Why it is new:** recruiting analytics looks backward at funnel averages. A forecast ledger makes
current uncertainty explicit and tests whether the team actually understands its missions.

**First test:** shadow-mode forecasts on fixtures or internal operations only. Never forecast candidate
success, worth, retention, or protected outcomes.

### 13.11 Aggregate Talent Observatory

**Combination:** privacy clean rooms + differential privacy + compensation benchmarks + market design.

Trusted firms could eventually learn aggregate facts such as the prevalence of a skill/location
combination, typical process duration, or compensation distribution without pooling identifiable
candidate records. Query restrictions, minimum cohorts, noise, audit logs, and purpose limits protect
individuals.

**First test:** research whether any useful aggregate question cannot be answered by public or licensed
non-person data. If no repeated need exists, do not build a clean room.

### 13.12 Mission Constitution

**Combination:** Cedar-style authorization + NIST AI risk profiles + Ramp exception routing + DIE's
authority receipts.

Every mission declares which principals may perform which actions on which resources under which
context. High-risk functions also declare required review, evidence, reversibility, and stop events.
The visible constitution makes agent and human authority inspectable at the point of work.

**First test:** render the existing hard-coded authority matrix for one role workspace. A policy
language is unnecessary until multiple real roles or tenants require differing policies.

## 14. Additional research questions created by the new connections

### 14.1 Mission twins and counterfactuals

52. Which role changes have predictable downstream dependencies, and which require human
    reinterpretation?
53. Can comparing two mission branches expose hidden disagreement earlier than editing one brief?
54. What is the smallest scenario representation that remains honest about uncertainty?
55. How should a branch record evidence that was unavailable in the live mission at the time?
56. When should a scenario be merged, archived, or explicitly rejected?

### 14.2 Candidate-controlled data

57. Which candidate-supplied sources are worth linking rather than uploading or entering manually?
58. Does source-linked verification reduce burden, or merely make surveillance feel official?
59. Can a useful workflow function when a candidate declines every optional connection?
60. What derived data must be deleted or recomputed when a grant is withdrawn?
61. How should a consent receipt communicate uncertainty about downstream retention and legal duties?

### 14.3 Common operating pictures

62. Which mission state is genuinely shared, and which must remain participant-specific?
63. Can “next safe action” outperform a generic task list in reducing stalled missions?
64. Which closure conditions prevent zombie roles and endless candidate limbo?
65. What deserves an incident-like retrospective: a failed intro, a bad data disclosure, a missed
    handoff, or every closed mission?
66. How should service recovery be recorded without permanently labeling a participant as difficult?

### 14.4 Reproducibility and provenance

67. What minimum provenance lets an operator reproduce a review without retaining unnecessary raw
    person data?
68. Which transformations are important enough to appear in an evidence bill of materials?
69. How should a human correction supersede an agent output while preserving both histories?
70. Can method version explain review differences better than model version alone?
71. Which research protocols transfer across role cohorts, and which should stay local?

### 14.5 Offers and negotiation

72. Which offer assumptions create the largest misunderstandings for startup candidates?
73. Can scenario comparison improve informed consent without anchoring or manipulating either side?
74. What company information must be verified before equity scenarios are useful?
75. How should DIE represent non-monetary terms without reducing them to a questionable utility score?
76. Which negotiation state can be shared mutually and which belongs only to each party?

### 14.6 Privacy-enhanced market learning

77. Which aggregate market questions are decision-relevant and cannot be answered by current sources?
78. What minimum cohort and privacy budget would be necessary to prevent singling out?
79. Could repeated queries reconstruct a person or company even when each result is aggregated?
80. Who controls query purpose, audits abuse, and decides when the collaboration ends?
81. Would the same goal be achieved more safely by candidates voluntarily publishing aggregate data?

### 14.7 Forecasting and calibration

82. Which operational questions have objective, timely resolution criteria?
83. Does explicit forecasting improve decisions, or add performative pressure and false precision?
84. How should forecasts be hidden or revealed to avoid anchoring reviewers?
85. Can calibration be learned across heterogeneous roles without pretending they are exchangeable?
86. When should a poor forecast change process policy rather than be treated as random error?

## 15. Expanded sequencing: what earns attention first

### 15.1 Strengthen the current vertical slice now

These concepts reuse current objects and can be tested with isolated fixtures:

1. **Evidence Bill of Materials:** derive it from existing receipts and run history.
2. **Decision Rehearsal:** preserve initial view, counterargument, decision-changing evidence, and
   final rationale.
3. **Mission Twin lite:** one reversible requirement-change comparison with explicit affected state.
4. **Hiring Case view:** render owner, objective, unresolved questions, waiting state, next safe action,
   and closure conditions from existing workspace data.
5. **Mutual projection check:** prove that operator-private and candidate-safe projections cannot leak
   into one another.

These five form a stronger Role Mission Decision Trace without requiring a new provider, database,
workflow engine, graph, policy language, or hosted multi-user product.

### 15.2 Research alongside the first real mission

- Candidate Data Link desirability and refusal-safe UX.
- Relationship Hospitality preferences and retention.
- Mission forecasts limited to process state.
- Compensation and offer assumption clarity.
- Whether a mutual diligence room reduces clarification loops.
- Whether retrospective state produces a reusable correction rather than narrative hindsight.

### 15.3 Build only after repeated evidence

- a formal authorization engine after real policy variation exceeds explicit code and tests;
- durable workflow infrastructure after a demonstrated lost, duplicated, or unrecoverable run;
- a graph/ontology substrate after two or more real surfaces require relationship traversal that
  current indexes cannot answer safely;
- privacy clean rooms after a valuable cross-firm aggregate query, trustworthy collaborators, and
  legal/privacy review exist;
- expert-marketplace mechanics after scoped human research repeatedly improves mission outcomes;
- offer integrations after sourced manual scenarios are useful and existing tools cannot supply the
  needed context.

### 15.4 Explicit non-directions

- no forecasting, tournament, or work-sample leaderboard as a candidate-worth score;
- no “digital twin of a person” or inferred psychological profile;
- no relationship graph built from scraped mailboxes, calendars, or contacts;
- no urgency/severity system that pressures candidates or equates hiring with incident response;
- no immutable consent claim—withdrawal, expiry, purpose change, and deletion remain first-class;
- no privacy-tech exception to purpose limitation, fairness, data minimization, or legal review;
- no general ontology, policy language, clean room, or workflow platform before observed need.

## Conclusion

The ambitious product is not a larger table and not a more autonomous recruiter. It is a mission-
centered system that combines evidence, human judgment, trusted relationships, consented
coordination, and outcome-linked learning without collapsing their authority boundaries.

The roadmap already points there. The next useful move is to make the destination legible in one
Role Mission Decision Trace, test it with isolated data now, and reserve product claims for the
first real accepted role. That keeps the vision large and the evidence honest.
