Four deliberate steps.

Systems keep the process organized. Judgment stays human. Your information remains private until there is a specific, strong match and both sides agree.

  1. 01

    Define the outcome

    Founders share the role, must-haves, and the one result this hire should deliver in 90 days.

  2. 02

    Human review

    We read the brief or profile and look for evidence of fit—not keyword overlap or application volume.

  3. 03

    Small proposal

    When fit is real, we explain why and ask each side privately. No lists. No profile blasts.

  4. 04

    Mutual yes

    Only then do we make a warm introduction. Startups pay 10% only when a hire starts.

After you press send.

Submitting starts a human review. It does not publish the role, accept commercial terms, charge a card, or trigger a candidate blast.

First

A person reads the context.

We check the outcome, role, compensation, location, and constraints. If something important is unclear, follow-up comes from hello@trydemigod.com. No response-time promise or candidate slate is implied.

Before an introduction

Each side decides privately.

A candidate’s name or profile is not shared with a startup until that candidate has approved the specific company and role and the startup also wants the conversation.

No card or deposit is collected with the brief. If a search moves forward, written commercial terms name the cash-salary figure to which the 10% fee applies.

“What must be true 90 days after this person starts?”

That answer is a cleaner matching anchor than a generic job description.

What this is not

A board, an ATS, or a recruiter blast.

Demigod is a narrow human-reviewed layer between SF startup needs and people who may actually want them. Candidates join free. Profiles stay private until mutual yes.
