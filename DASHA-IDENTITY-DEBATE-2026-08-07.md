# Debate — should the identity move from the name to the cherry?

2026-08-07. Claude proposed, Codex and Grok were asked to attack it. Both returned "half-right" and
both, independently, named the same first move — which is the useful part of the result.

## The proposition

> Dasha has two problems with one shared solution. **(A)** Another token — Dasha (VVAIFU), larger and
> listed on CoinGecko, CoinMarketCap, Coinbase, OKX and Bitget — owns the name on every major
> surface. **(B)** The name is tied to a real person who never endorsed it, which is a
> right-of-publicity exposure and the reason the site keeps having to disclaim things. The shared
> solution: move the identity's centre of gravity from the person's name to the **cherry** — the
> mark, the character, the CC0 kit. The ticker stays `$dasha` because it cannot change on-chain, but
> what the project *is* becomes the cherry.

## What survived

**The half both kept:** stop leaning on a real person's identity as the product spine, and build a
mark that can be owned without permission drama. The cherry is right *as a visual system*.

**The half both killed:** that A and B share one fix.

- **On A** — Grok: the collision is "surfaces + size", not naming. VVAIFU wins search because it is
  larger, listed and indexed, "not because your logo is a name. A fruit doesn't dethrone 'Dasha' on
  Google/CG/CMC." The real differentiator between colliding tickers is the contract address.
- **On B** — publicity risk attaches to *commercial use of a person's identity*. It is not cured by
  adding a mascot while the ticker still says `$dasha` and the copy still says Dasha. A mascot
  reduces exposure; it does not erase it.
- **On the research I leaned on** — Grok's correction is the sharpest line in the exchange: the
  symbolism finding means *name + symbol + story after discovery*, not "replace the name with
  produce." I had over-read it.

## The first move: both said the same thing

**Fix the token's own metadata, via the DexScreener community-takeover route.** Not listings, not
Jupiter, not the identity shift, not more product.

The argument, in Codex's words: the defect is not weak branding, it is **broken provenance**. The
token's primary public card sends people to a dead domain and a Telegram the project disclaims,
while omitting the actual product. Everything else — aggregator listings, Jupiter verification, a
rebrand, more Studio features — *amplifies whatever is on that card*. Grok put the same point
harder: anyone who has already found the mint is being actively misled right now.

This costs money and makes a public claim. It is the user's call, not an agent's.

## Two blind spots they found

**1. Authority (Codex).** The strongest objection in the exchange, and I did not see it at all.

> "'Our site,' 'the project,' and 'community takeover' may currently describe aspiration rather than
> demonstrable mandate from the token deployer or holders… Otherwise the cherry shift is not merely a
> bait-and-switch risk; it could be a second unaffiliated identity attaching itself to the same mint."

We have been reasoning as though getdasha.com self-evidently represents `$dasha`. Nothing
establishes that. Claiming a takeover without a basis for speaking for the token is the same
category of move the project exists to be better than. **Fix identity control before identity
design.**

**2. The war we may not need to fight (Grok).**

> "'Dasha coin' search may never have been *your* traffic — you may be fighting a war you don't need
> if the product is Studio/CC0 and the mint is a footnote found by contract."

Memecoin discovery mostly runs on contract addresses pasted into posts, not on name search. If that
is true here, the VVAIFU collision costs approximately nothing and I ranked it far too high this
afternoon on an assumption I never tested.

## What this changes

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| CoinGecko + CMC listings first | **Metadata/provenance first**; listings amplify whatever the card says |
| Name collision is a top-three problem | Probably minor — untested assumption about how people actually arrive |
| Cherry identity solves collision *and* publicity risk | Cherry is a visual system. It solves neither; it reduces one |
| We are the project | **Unestablished.** Mandate is a precondition, not a given |

## Question 1 — answered by the user, 2026-08-07

**The project is official.** Developed by John Potter ([x.com/potterlab](https://x.com/potterlab)),
working directly with Dasha and with [@perryalpha](https://x.com/perryalpha).

This resolves Codex's strongest objection: the mandate exists, and the "second unaffiliated identity"
risk does not apply. It also removes the precondition that was gating everything — the DexScreener
community-takeover route, the listings and Jupiter verification are all now questions of *when and
how much to spend*, not *whether we have standing*.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **The CTO framing is wrong and should be dropped.** A community takeover describes an abandoned
   token adopted by outsiders. That is not this. The DexScreener metadata still needs fixing, but as
   the operator correcting a stale record — not as a community claiming a vacancy.
2. **Some existing disclaimer copy may now be inaccurate in the other direction.** The live footer
   says "Dasha Nekrasova did not make this site. References do not imply authorization or
   endorsement." If the project works directly with her, part of that may understate the
   relationship. **It has not been changed**, because the gap between "works directly with",
   "authorized by" and "endorsed by" is exactly where public claims go wrong, and the wording needs
   to be precisely what is true rather than approximately generous. This needs one clarifying
   sentence from the operator, not an agent's guess.

## Remaining open questions

1. **How do people actually arrive at this token?** If it is contract-address-first, the whole
   aggregator strategy drops in priority. Currently assumed, not measured.
2. **What exactly may be claimed publicly** about the relationship with Dasha — see above.
3. Then: listings, Jupiter verification, and how far the cherry carries the identity.
