# Dasha participatory culture research

**Updated:** 2026-08-08  
**Status:** Current research decision; informs the brief and roadmap, but does not authorize a build or public claim.  
**Question:** What is the smallest Dasha product that can turn attention into recurring, community-authored culture without making trading, accounts or a leaderboard the product?

**Academic source map:** [`DASHA-ACADEMIC-EVIDENCE.md`](DASHA-ACADEMIC-EVIDENCE.md) groups the underlying studies by evidence design, limitation and Dasha transfer.

## Decision in one paragraph

Test **Dasha Transmissions**: one bounded, in-character situation with a supplied visual starter, a short creative task, an immediate Studio path, manual submission, an acknowledgement for every first contribution and a small curated result. Selected responses may influence a second transmission. This is weaker and more testable than committing to a social network, AI companion, DAO, automated Simp leaderboard or token-gated platform. The first test uses the existing website, Studio, GitHub and a manually operated community channel; it adds no wallet connection, account system, database, automated X scoring or financial reward.

## Third research pass — Grok convergence and final selection

Grok completed an independent 30-track review covering small-community retention, remix systems, recognition, memecoin survival, provenance, AI-assisted creation, governance, moderation, licensing and open-source incentives. The raw model output is preserved as [`DASHA-GROK-DEEP-RESEARCH-2026-08-08.md`](DASHA-GROK-DEEP-RESEARCH-2026-08-08.md); it is evidence input, not product authority.

Its decisive conclusions converge with the earlier passes:

- a first valid contribution being seen is better-supported than points, ranks or elaborate praise;
- a hyperspecific identity and bounded role are plausible advantages for a small community, but the supporting small-community study is qualitative and does not prove Dasha retention;
- chained creative systems can produce deep remix behavior, but Dasha has no evidence yet that recipients will preserve its editable link;
- image metadata is not a reliable cross-platform carrier because platform handling is inconsistent and mutable; the companion fragment URL remains Dasha's practical editability carrier;
- public `$dasha` attention appears price-forward, so creative demand must be created and measured rather than inferred from cashtag activity;
- recognition may increase volume while narrowing novelty, making a universal Simp rank a poor first experiment.

### Final product selection

The product experiment is **Dasha Transmissions**. Studio is its instrument. Remix Relay is the critical behavior under observation. A static archive and the Simp Board are possible consequences of real participation, not parallel acquisition loops.

Transmission 001 uses the working instruction **`make me an alibi.`**, an original deliberately incomplete starter, a seven-day window, acknowledgement by the end of the next calendar day and one editorial closing record. No second Transmission is scheduled until the first produces pull.

This selection makes fewer commitments than every surviving alternative: it needs no account, feed, database, wallet, AI persona, automated ingestion, public rank, token reward, Discord dependency or claim of official canon. It can distinguish one-shot Studio utility from recurring ritual and can observe whether editable state survives an actual handoff.

## Evidence ledger

### Directly observed in Dasha

- Home, Studio and Desk already provide a front door, a browser-native creation tool and a trust layer.
- Studio already preserves editable state in a fragment URL and exports portable images.
- The public Desk repository is static, open source and verified before Pages deployment.
- Existing strategy documents contain several adjacent ideas—Relay, Capsules, archive, Discord, Simp Board and open culture objects—but no single recurring participation ritual owns the next experiment.
- There is no evidence yet that strangers repeatedly create, share or remix Dasha artifacts. Feature completion is not demand evidence.

### External evidence

1. **A shared starter plus a small prompt lowers the cost of participation.** DS106's Daily Create designs exercises for roughly 15–20 minutes with no registration or prizes. Adobe's 2026 Remix challenge gives participants the same starting image and asks for different interpretations. These examples support a bounded prompt, not a blank creation surface.  
   Sources: [DS106 Daily Create](https://daily.ds106.us/about/) · [Adobe Remix It, May 2026](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-678/may-26-remix-it-challenge-1561793/index2.html)

2. **A recurring character becomes more engaging when it presents situations and invites community input.** Research on storytelling community agents describes a loop of character/world definition, live situations and community response; its small pilot supports the direction but does not establish general product-market fit.  
   Source: [Storytelling Community AI Agents](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S187595212500028X)

3. **Recognition can motivate creation, but rank position changes the experience.** Social recognition is a meaningful creative incentive. Leaderboard studies show mixed contextual effects: visible progress can increase short-term contribution, while negative placement can be worse than no feedback and absolute ranking intensifies comparison. Rewards in a professional community have also displaced knowledge-sharing motives with reward-seeking. The safe inference is to recognize several kinds of contribution before introducing one total order.  
   Sources: [Social Reward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09872) · [Leaderboard feedback study, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025002122) · [Absolute and relative leaderboards](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131521001743) · [Rewards in the SAP community](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/tkmrxx/v5y2007i3p186-198.html)

4. **Remix systems should carry source and license information with the artifact.** Creative Commons recommends automated Title, Author, Source and License attribution at download and when a work enters a remix tool. Attribution must not imply endorsement.  
   Sources: [CC Platform Toolkit](https://creativecommons.org/platform/toolkit/) · [Creative Commons licenses](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/use-remix/cc-licenses/)

5. **Community spaces lose newcomers when their first action is unclear.** Discord's own onboarding guidance emphasizes a small set of useful default channels, short choices and a direct answer to “what would you like to do here?” It warns that too many channels, roles and verification steps overwhelm newcomers.  
   Sources: [Discord Community Onboarding](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/11074987197975-Community-Onboarding-FAQ) · [Discord Server Guide](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/13497665141655-Server-Guide-FAQ)

6. **Open-source participation depends on relationships and comprehension, not merely contribution volume.** GitHub's 2026 mentorship discussion warns that easy AI-generated submissions can strain maintainers and argues for conversations that establish understanding. GitHub's contributor tooling emphasizes new and returning contributors, recognition and maintainer development rather than raw commit totals alone.  
   Sources: [Open-source mentorship in the AI era](https://github.blog/open-source/maintainers/rethinking-open-source-mentorship-in-the-ai-era/) · [Contributor insights](https://github.blog/open-source/maintainers/how-to-gain-insight-into-your-project-contributors/)

7. **Crypto creator products increasingly collapse creation into trading.** Zora makes posts, profiles and trends tradeable and pays creators from trading activity. That validates attention around creator economics but also leaves room for an experience in which cultural participation is useful before any trade.  
   Source: [Zora product changes](https://support.zora.co/en/articles/4641857)

8. **Community IP can expand a character when reuse rules and assets are clear.** Pudgy Penguins supplies brand materials and a project-submission path; other character projects publish explicit derivative-work boundaries. This supports a compact Dasha creation kit, but Dasha's use of a real person's name and likeness requires narrower claims than an owned fictional collection.  
   Sources: [Pudgy community brand builder](https://media.pudgypenguins.com/comic) · [Roblox IP licensing for creators](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ip-licensing/creators)

9. **Solana memecoin activity is abundant and risky; token success research does not prove a culture product causes financial performance.** Recent studies examine very large token-launch datasets and social-presence effects, but their endpoints concern launch/graduation behavior. They do not establish that adding a community feature increases durable value, and Dasha must not use them to imply returns.  
   Sources: [Predicting token success on Pump.fun](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860) · [Pump.fun survival analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823)

## Second research pass — recurrence, cold start and differentiation

### Findings that revise the first pass

1. **Acknowledgement matters more reliably than the tone or length of the acknowledgement.** In a study of 2,777 newcomers across six public newsgroups, receiving any reply increased the observed probability of posting again from 44% to 56%. The hypotheses that positivity, agreement, informativeness or reply length independently improved return were not supported. Dasha should guarantee that a first submission is seen and answered, but it should not turn exaggerated praise into a retention mechanism.  
   Source: [Predicting Continued Participation in Newsgroups](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/3/723/4617705)

2. **Ritual requires shared symbols and active roles, not merely a calendar.** Recent qualitative work on livestream communities links recurring greetings, symbolic objects, insider language and moderator mediation with collective identity. Broader online-ritual research also treats repeated mediated encounters as capable of sustaining belonging. These studies use narrow settings and cannot prove that a weekly Dasha prompt will retain users; they support designing a recognizable opening, role and closing rather than simply posting on schedule.  
   Sources: [Digital Rituals in Live Streaming Communities](https://ejournal.grafindoscience.com/jcc/article/view/182) · [Belonging online](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/165694/)

3. **Rigid canon is not required for a coherent collaborative world.** Collaborative-fandom research describes believable shared concepts and community “fanon” that can coexist with personal variations instead of forcing every contribution into one official continuity. Dasha has an additional authority problem because the name and likeness refer to a real person. The safe early model is sourced fact, project continuity and community fiction—not a claim that the project controls official canon.  
   Sources: [Collaborative fanfiction and hyperdiegesis](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0787/11/4/87) · [Canon and fanon in collaborative fan works](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S8755461525000246)

4. **Cold starts need enough initial material to make participation legible, but seeded work is not community demand.** Research defines cold start as the absence of an initial mass of useful content and finds that motivations change as communities mature. Dasha should seed a few clearly labeled examples showing the allowed range, then report operator and non-operator artifacts separately.  
   Source: [Motivational differences across online-community stages](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378720624001320)

5. **Participation inequality is normal, but the 90–9–1 ratio is not a law.** Empirical studies find strong contribution concentration while also showing that exact proportions vary materially by community. Passive viewing can be a valid early stage; success should be measured as movement between stages and the breadth of creators, not by expecting most visitors to submit.  
   Sources: [The 1% Rule in four digital health networks](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1438887114002428) · [Behavioral evolution and the 90–9–1 rule](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/1949664)

6. **Guidance before submission can improve quality and reduce moderation work.** A large Reddit field experiment found that contextual post guidance increased posts surviving moderation, improved contribution quality by its engagement measure and reduced manually reviewed reports, without changing later posting activity. A Dasha submission path should show concise source, permission and format requirements before—not after—someone creates.  
   Source: [Post Guidance for Online Communities](https://doi.org/10.1145/3711046)

7. **Showing only exceptionally polished references may suppress rather than elevate later work.** An observational analysis across Cities: Skylines, SCP Wiki and Archive of Our Own found non-linear relationships between reference quality diversity and later output quality; extremely high-quality references were associated with worse subsequent output quality. This does not establish causation, but it argues against launching with an intimidating wall of flawless operator art. Show a small, moderately varied set of honest examples.  
   Source: [Reference products and creative activity](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-65124-y)

8. **A prompt, challenge or remix feed is no longer differentiated.** Current products already offer one prompt per day for small friend groups, infinite feeds of remixable interactive objects, and fan platforms organized around creative challenges and rewards. Dasha's possible wedge is the combination of a specific editorial character, a bounded ritual, human acknowledgement, portable editable state and provenance.  
   Sources: [Social — one prompt a day](https://usesocial.app/) · [Sekai](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/sekai-create-remix-play/id6504506653) · [HEEBOO](https://www.heeboo.com/)

9. **Financial and social activity are unsafe proxies for cultural participation.** Meme-coin research documents fabricated comments, wash trading and other manipulation that can attract traders; NFT research similarly shows that incentive structures can make reported volume profitable to manufacture. Dasha product evaluation must therefore exclude token price, market cap, trade volume, follower count and raw social reactions from its experiment pass condition.  
   Sources: [Market manipulation in on-chain meme coins](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220651/) · [NFT wash-trading detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01543)

### Revised product implication

A Transmission is not “content posted every week.” It is a small interaction ritual with six required moments:

1. **signal:** a recognizable Dasha opening and transmission identifier;
2. **role:** one clear thing the visitor can contribute;
3. **starter:** enough material to remove blank-page friction;
4. **witness:** acknowledgement that the first contribution was received;
5. **closure:** a bounded ending and curated record;
6. **continuity:** a visible consequence, callback or next situation when participation earns one.

If any of these is absent, the test may measure content distribution or tool usage rather than community ritual.

## Live hypotheses

| Hypothesis | What it predicts | Extra commitments | Cheapest useful test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1: Creation utility** | People want a fast Dasha image tool but not an ongoing story | Studio quality and distribution | Observe independent exports and shares from one strong starter |
| **H2: Recurring character ritual** | A situation involving Dasha produces responses and return interest | Editorial cadence and light curation | Run one Transmission and only continue if a second is pulled by participation |
| **H3: Status community** | Recognition is the main reason people contribute | Identity, scoring, adjudication and anti-abuse rules | Manually feature contributions; measure whether recognition causes repeat work before scoring |
| **H4: Holder club** | Token ownership is the main source of belonging | Wallet proof, privacy, legal review and financialized hierarchy | Do not test first; ask only after non-financial participation exists |
| **H5: Open character platform** | Other communities want Dasha's tools and format | Generalization, SDK and support | Wait for one external community to request or independently reuse the format |

## Weakest sufficient hypothesis

The least restrictive plan consistent with current evidence is:

> People may participate when Dasha gives them one clear, low-effort creative role using a finished starter, and when good responses receive visible, human recognition.

This does not assume that they want accounts, a feed, an AI relationship, financial rewards, governance or an enduring platform. One Transmission can distinguish H1 from H2 and cheaply probe H3.

## Product concept: Dasha Transmissions

A Transmission is a small, time-bounded episode expressed in Dasha's concise voice. It contains:

- one line of situation or instruction;
- one supplied image or editable Studio starter;
- one obvious action taking minutes;
- one public identifier, such as `transmission-001`;
- one submission path with clear reuse terms;
- one closing time;
- a small curated result or response from Dasha.

Example prompts:

- make me an alibi
- someone deleted Tuesday
- finish the message before he sees it
- design a warning label for me
- choose what I wear to court
- what happened here

The examples are voice sketches, not approved public copy.

## First experiment

### Scope

1. Put one Transmission on Home.
2. Open Studio with a finished starter and transmission identifier.
3. Preserve the identifier in the editable artifact and exported share version.
4. Accept submissions through one manually operated path.
5. Acknowledge every distinct person's first valid submission within a stated response window.
6. Curate a small result without pretending selection is objective.
7. Publish a second Transmission only if the first produces credible non-operator participation or unusually strong qualitative pull.

### Do not build for this test

- accounts or profiles;
- a database or general feed;
- an AI Dasha chatbot;
- automated X ingestion;
- wallet connection or holder scoring;
- token rewards, referrals or airdrop implications;
- on-chain voting;
- permanent canon machinery;
- a generalized creator SDK.

### Evidence that counts

- distinct non-operator people complete artifacts;
- artifacts materially differ from the starter;
- creators voluntarily share or submit them;
- at least one participant returns, remixes another artifact or asks for the next prompt;
- submissions show more than one mode of participation: image, line, code, documentation, curation or moderation.
- valid first submissions receive acknowledgement rather than disappearing into an intake queue.

### Evidence that does not count

- operator-made examples;
- bot reactions;
- compliments without creation;
- clicks into Studio without an export or meaningful edit;
- nominations manufactured to populate a leaderboard;
- impressions, token price, market cap, trade volume, follower growth or raw reactions.

### Pass, adapt and stop rules

- **Pass:** at least five distinct non-operator submissions, at least three materially changed artifacts and at least one repeat/remix signal within the bounded test.
- **Adapt once:** participation exists but users fail at the same obvious step; fix that shared friction and repeat once.
- **Stop:** fewer than two credible non-operator artifacts after real distribution, or participants consistently save/view without editing. Do not add rewards to rescue a failed creative loop.

These thresholds are working defaults for a first small test, not universal product metrics.

### Measurement funnel

Record small counts and timestamps rather than a composite score:

1. Transmission view;
2. Studio start from the Transmission;
3. material edit rather than unchanged export;
4. export;
5. voluntary submission or share intent;
6. acknowledgement delivered;
7. return, second contribution or remix;
8. independent contribution to the next Transmission.

Also record:

- distinct creators, separated from operator examples;
- time to first acknowledgement;
- percentage of first contributors receiving a response;
- concentration of submissions among the most active contributors;
- number of contributions with complete source/permission metadata;
- number of cross-person remixes;
- qualitative reasons for stopping when a participant volunteers them.

Do not create a single “community health” number. CHAOSS likewise treats newcomer experience as a combination of subsequent contribution, time between engagements, documentation and qualitative experience rather than one universal rank.  
Source: [CHAOSS Newcomer Experience](https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-newcomer-experience/)

## Recognition system

Use recognition before ranking:

- **Accomplices:** selected creative work
- **Mechanics:** merged code
- **Archivists:** documentation, sourcing and attribution
- **Witnesses:** useful moderation and community help
- **Believers:** optional, private-by-default holder verification later

Design rules:

- show what the person did, not only a number;
- allow several people and contribution types to be visible at once;
- keep selection editorial and disclosed during the experiment;
- never award raw reaction, reply, tag or posting volume;
- never let bag size determine cultural rank;
- give contributors a correction/removal path;
- prefer seasons or showcases to a permanent global hierarchy;
- retain permanent achievements only when supported by durable evidence.

The existing “Simp Board” can remain a playful presentation layer. It must not claim automated measurement until identity, collection, scoring and appeal systems actually exist.

## Character continuity model

Do not call the early archive official canon. Use provenance-first labels:

- **sourced:** a factual public source is linked and the summary stays within it;
- **project continuity:** selected by the getdasha project for a later Transmission callback;
- **community fiction:** clearly transformative or fictional participant work;
- **remix:** a derivative artifact with its immediate source and permission state preserved.

“Project continuity” means only that getdasha may refer to the contribution again. It does not imply approval, authorship or endorsement by the real person. Playful labels such as “denied by Dasha” remain unsafe unless the entire surface unmistakably establishes a fictional community narrator.

## Community architecture

The product loop must work on the website before Discord becomes required. When a server exists:

- default view should answer “what is happening now?” and “what can I make?”;
- use a few active channels before specialized rooms;
- make the current Transmission the onboarding task;
- keep coin discussion separate from creative submissions;
- publish moderation standards before introducing rewards;
- document how selections, removals and disputes work;
- promote moderators from observed contributors rather than handing out status for recruitment alone.

## Artifact and licensing requirements

Every submitted or remixed artifact should be able to carry:

- title or transmission identifier;
- maker-supplied display name;
- immediate source URL or remix parent when present;
- source asset attribution;
- applicable license or permission state;
- indication that changes were made;
- a non-endorsement boundary.

The system must distinguish:

1. original Dasha project assets that the project can license;
2. contributor-owned submissions licensed for reuse;
3. third-party material used under a compatible license;
4. public posts or likeness material that may be referenced but is not automatically relicensable.

CC0 is appropriate only for material the contributor or project actually has authority to dedicate. It cannot erase third-party likeness, trademark, publicity or source-asset rights.

## Token boundary

The coin can support discovery and symbolism without controlling participation.

Safe early relationship:

- Desk provides exact-mint verification and neutral third-party routes;
- Home keeps risk disclosure near coin actions;
- creative participation remains available without purchase;
- holder status, if later proven privately, provides a small badge or bounded cosmetic—not vote dominance or rank proportional to balance;
- no content reward is described as yield, return, dividend or expected appreciation;
- product metrics and token price remain separate.

## Failure modes and countermeasures

| Failure mode | Early signal | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Empty-room theater | Only operators submit | Stop or change the prompt once; never fabricate members |
| Generic meme sludge | Outputs differ only by caption | Stronger starter, tighter constraint, editorial selection |
| Dasha voice becomes corporate | Long explanatory prompts | One situation, one action, few words |
| Character impersonation | Copy presents fiction as her statement | Label community-created fiction; never claim endorsement |
| Reward farming | Many low-effort submissions | No volume points; curate quality and contribution diversity |
| Winner lock-in | Same accounts dominate every showcase | Rotate categories, seasons and newcomer spots without fake equality |
| Copyright ambiguity | Missing source or unknown upload rights | Require source/permission state and preserve attribution |
| Coin swallows product | Every action routes to buy | Keep creation complete without wallet or purchase |
| Moderation overload | Submission volume exceeds review capacity | Bounded windows and manual caps before feeds or automation |
| Lore incoherence | Every artifact claims canon | Use canon / possibly true / community fiction labels |
| Tool overbuild | Accounts and feeds precede participation | Require behavior evidence at every expansion gate |

## Roadmap implications

1. Keep Studio as the creation engine and Desk as the trust engine.
2. Test one Transmission before expanding Relay, Capsules, Simp scoring or Discord automation.
3. If the Transmission works, add a static archive page before a feed.
4. If people remix one another, strengthen lineage and portable attribution.
5. If recognition causes repeat participation, formalize contribution categories and an appeal/removal path.
6. Only after recurring activity should the project consider profiles, automation, holder cosmetics, live rooms or a generalized format.

## Open questions

- Does the current Dasha audience want to create, collect, observe, trade or converse?
- Which starter material may be legally remixed and redistributed?
- Who has authority to select “canon” or speak in the project's fictional Dasha voice?
- Which submission channel can be moderated consistently?
- Can the first experiment be distributed to real non-operator participants?
- Does an in-character continuation outperform a conventional gallery announcement?
- Will editable links survive sharing, or will people keep only flattened images?
- Does public recognition produce a second contribution without points?
- What evidence would justify treating the project as a community stewardship effort rather than an unofficial fan tool?

## Research conclusion

The product opportunity is not “a memecoin with utility.” It is a small cultural ritual around a recognizable character, with portable tools and honest provenance. The project should earn complexity in this order:

**prompt → creation → share → recognition → return → remix → archive → identity → rewards**

Skipping directly to identity, rewards or governance would measure the incentive system before proving that the underlying activity is worth doing.
