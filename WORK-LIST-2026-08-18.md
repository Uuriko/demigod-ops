# 75 things to work on, and the research that reorders them

Written after measuring both sites and reading what the market actually pays for. Two findings
below should change what we build, not just what we do next.

## The two research findings that matter

**1. Depth is not where this wins, and I had that wrong.** Lightcast holds US postings back to
January 2010. LinkUp has indexed daily since 2007. Coresignal reaches 2020. The archive
reconstruction I built today gets a few hundred SF boards back to roughly 2020–2022 — which places
us behind the incumbents by a decade on the axis I was treating as the prize. Enterprise deals in
this category are four-to-six-figure engagements against exactly those datasets.

What we hold that they do not publish is the **refusal discipline**: an unread board is never a zero,
a closure is only recorded when observed, a company that rewrote its own posted date is counted
(129 roles), and a board that will not name its owner is never attributed. Nobody sells that,
because it makes your numbers smaller. It is the only defensible claim here, and it is a *method*
claim rather than a *volume* claim.

**2. A dataset is not a moat; a feedback loop is.** "Volume proves nothing… the flywheel is the
moat, not the dataset," and the data that counts is "the data the public corpus lacks." Ours does
not compound, because nothing uses it. Every hour spent widening the corpus while zero people touch
the product is an hour spent on the axis that is explicitly *not* defensible.

That reorders everything below: **§A before §B, always.**
