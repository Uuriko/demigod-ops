# The Dasha kit is CC0

**Updated: 2026-08-07.**

## What this covers

The following are dedicated to the public domain under
[CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/):

- the Dasha mark — `dasha-mark.svg`, `dasha-favicon.svg`, and the PNG renders of them;
- the Studio's looks — Poster, Ticket, Printout, Marquee, Signal — and the code that draws them;
- **every image and GIF the Studio exports**, including the ones you make.

You may copy, change, sell, print, remix and build on any of it, commercially or not, without asking
and without credit. There is no application, no allowlist and nobody to email.

The dedication is irrevocable. Work already released under it stays released.

## Why

Nouns, mfers and Milady are all CC0, and it is not incidental to how they spread — derivative work
multiplies the parent brand instead of competing with it. A visual identity that needs permission
does not proliferate, because the permission step is where people stop. The cheapest way to have a
culture is to stop guarding it.

## What CC0 does not do

CC0 waives copyright. It does not touch two other things, and pretending otherwise would be worse
than not doing this at all:

**Trademark.** The dedication is not permission to present your work as issued by Dasha, to imply it
is official, or to use the name and mark as the badge of a different token or product. Remix freely;
do not impersonate.

**Publicity and personality rights.** CC0 has no effect on the rights of any person depicted.
Nothing here is permission to use Dasha Nekrasova's name, likeness or identity, or to imply her
authorization, participation or endorsement — the project has never had it, and no licence of ours
could grant it.

Two further things this cannot cover, because they were never ours to give: any third-party image
you bring to a remix, and the accuracy of anything you write on one. A CC0 mark on an image does not
make a claim inside that image true, and it is not investment advice from anyone.

## How to apply it to your own derivative

You do not have to do anything. If you want to be explicit:

> This work is dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0 Universal.
