# Dasha pivot decision

> Superseded forecasting-era decision. Historical research only; do not implement.

Updated: 2026-08-06

## Decision

Do not make a full company or technology pivot yet. Make a **wedge pivot**:

- from solo call cards for alpha broadcasters;
- to host-led structured disagreement and resolution for crypto media and communities.

Keep the underlying mission: make consequential claims inspectable before outcomes. Change the first customer and first recurring use case.

The recommended initial product is a **Resolvable Receipt**, followed by **Dasha Pair** and a manual **Dasha Round**:

1. one bounded, falsifiable call;
2. one server-received time and stable public URL;
3. one horizon, invalidation condition and resolution reminder;
4. one later outcome and postmortem;
5. then independent challenges and hosted rounds built on the same record.

## Why not a full pivot

The current thesis has not been tested with the roadmap's target cohort. There is no retention, sharing or comprehension evidence that falsifies it; there is mostly build evidence. Abandoning it now would be idea churn rather than a response to user behavior.

Existing assets transfer directly to structured disagreement:

- working no-wallet Thesis Card;
- share-card generator;
- falsification and horizon fields;
- truth/proof boundaries;
- editorial landing design;
- research on predecisional accountability and belief updating;
- Dasha Desk and planned Discord distribution surfaces.

The wedge pivot adds one new behavior instead of discarding the product.

## Why change the wedge

Solo crypto-call records are increasingly crowded:

- Thesis combines token theses, votes and reputation.
- Zaps detects calls, connects them to community trades and settles outcomes.
- PumpScope and XSCAN track KOL calls and performance.
- KOL Verdict, Kaito and others score reputation or attention.

A local checksum and attractive card are useful interface components, not a defensible category.

At the same time, prediction markets are becoming a major media format. New products and media companies are forming around market interpretation, while mainstream platforms are adding prediction experiences. This validates demand for future-oriented content but makes operating another market or generic analysis publication unattractive.

The remaining opening is the reasoning between a social claim and a market probability: opposing assumptions, sources, revisions and postmortems.

### Fresh competitive check — 2026-08-06

- PredictStamp already offers no-wallet public forecasts, timestamps, final results, reputation and direct two-person challenges. Generic “receipt + challenge + score” is therefore not an open category.
- CallScore tracks crypto commentary against market candles and exposes a correction/recompute path. This reinforces that mapping an ambiguous claim to a resolution source is a core product problem, not backend plumbing.
- Hunchproof presents cryptographic commit/reveal and scoring, so sealing alone is not a differentiated user benefit.
- Ember's public archive and correction history show that a locked prediction record can still produce a wrong outcome when its resolver or source pipeline fails.

Weakest surviving wedge: make the exact resolution contract legible—metric, source, cutoff/timezone and fallback—while keeping the record portable and accountless. First observe this manually during the capped receipt beta. Add a Source-Lock Challenge only if the clarification behavior appears; do not infer demand from competitor gaps alone.

## Feature order

### 1. Resolvable Receipt

Turn the existing card into a stable public record with a server-received time, canonical URL, resolution date and later outcome/postmortem. Include a calendar reminder. This is the minimum return loop and the prerequisite for credible challenges.

Do not add accounts, wallets, a feed or on-chain infrastructure.

### 2. Dasha Pair

Add “Challenge this thesis” to a public record. The challenger creates a separately sealed opposing record that references the original; do not let one browser claim two independent authors.

Preserve the question and horizon, collect an opposing thesis and invalidation, then export one paired image.

### 3. Example Pair

Put one realistic paired example on the landing page. It must show substantive disagreement, not generic bull/bear copy.

### 4. Manual Round mode

Add a host prompt, cutoff and space for 5–10 locally entered submissions. Generate a reveal mosaic. Run the first round manually before building host administration.

### 5. Resolution and postmortem card

Let a host mark a bounded outcome as resolved, invalidated, expired or disputed and attach a short source-backed explanation. Preserve the original pair.

### 6. Evidence cutoff

Allow up to three primary-source links with retrieval time and a clear distinction between evidence available at commitment and evidence learned later.

### 7. Private-until-cutoff reveal

Only after repeated hosted-round use, hide submissions until a stated cutoff. Reuse the public-record model; do not build a separate system.

## Features worth adding later

- Fork a thesis without copying its rationale
- “I changed my mind” update cards
- Honest-miss and invalidation cards
- Pair-to-pair comparison across time
- Host-branded recap export
- Calendar reminder for resolution
- Public JSON export
- Conflict or sponsorship disclosure
- Calibration for well-defined binary questions only
- Reader-to-new-pair flow

## Pivots to keep available

### A. Crypto claim-provenance publication

Map primary sources, paraphrases, incentives and changes behind viral claims. Strong editorial and brand fit; moderate sourcing and liability burden.

Trigger: people read and share source maps but do not create their own calls.

### B. Source-of-truth desks for communities

Offer official links, contract state, known unknowns, incident updates and change history. The current Dasha Desk is a reference implementation.

Trigger: repeated requests from communities to reproduce the Desk and measurable repeat direct visits.

### C. Prediction-market research companion

Add qualitative reasoning and evidence history around existing markets without wagering.

Trigger: hosts value Dasha recaps but participants prefer existing markets for probability entry.

### D. Agent decision receipts

Make an agent's proposed action, authority, constraints and result legible.

Trigger: direct demand from agent builders. Do not enter now: multiple dedicated receipt, governance and audit-trail products already exist.

### E. Tokenized-asset explainer

Explain issuer, custodian, redemption, jurisdiction and wrapper risks.

Trigger: a credible distribution partner and legal review. Otherwise the operational burden exceeds current fit.

## Pivots to reject now

- New chain or attestation protocol
- Wallet or exchange
- Stablecoin or payments infrastructure
- Prediction market
- KOL leaderboard
- Automated trading or AI alpha
- Token launchpad
- Generic fact checker
- Social feed
- Token-gated Discord

These are crowded, capital-intensive, regulated, difficult to distribute or inconsistent with Dasha's trust advantage.

## Pivot gates

Run three progressively stronger tests before changing the company direction.

### Test 1: paired artifact

At least 20 target users or 10 real pairs.

Continue if:

- 50% can explain the artifact without coaching;
- 30% begin a counter-thesis after seeing an original;
- 50% of started counter-theses finish;
- 25% of completed pairs open share intent;
- participants describe the value as disagreement, clarity or accountability rather than a signal.

### Test 2: manual hosted rounds

Three rounds across at least two independent hosts.

Continue if:

- each round gets at least five submissions;
- host setup remains under ten minutes;
- more than half of records reach resolution;
- at least 30% of participants return for reveal or resolution;
- one host requests another round without payment.

### Test 3: retention

Continue toward sealed records only if at least 20% of creators make another record within 14 days or repeatedly participate in host rounds.

## Full-pivot conditions

Pivot away from the thesis/round product if either condition occurs:

1. The paired artifact fails comprehension or creation thresholds after two materially different presentation tests.
2. Hosts can recruit submissions but participants do not return for reveal/resolution, showing that the artifact is disposable content rather than a recurring product.

Then choose based on observed pull:

- source-map consumption → claim-provenance publication;
- repeated community verification requests → source-of-truth desks;
- existing-market integration demand → prediction-market companion;
- agent-builder demand → agent decision receipts.

Do not choose a pivot because its market is fashionable. Choose the adjacent behavior users already performed without prompting.

## Current strategic statement

> Dasha makes crypto calls accountable: publish the claim before the outcome, return for the result, and challenge it with a separate record.

This is narrower and more testable than “version control for conviction,” while preserving that broader long-term category.
